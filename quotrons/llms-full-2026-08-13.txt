## 1. Current status

V1 is permanently retired after a stale-approval exploit affecting its
ERC-721 mirror. V2 is a separate collection and contract stack; it does
not replace or mutate the V1 collection.

The verified V2 contracts are deployed on Robinhood Chain, chain id
4663. The snapshot-based zero-action distribution is finalized: eligible
V1 dark terminals, hardwired terminals, fractional balances, and approved
restitution allocations were delivered directly to V2 wallets.

The canonical QUOTRON/WETH pool is seeded. The token and hook are open,
trading and hardwiring are live, and launch fees are permanently
finalized at 3%. The website continues to read those onchain gates and
fails closed; it does not rely on a date countdown.

The collection is revealed and its assets are pinned to IPFS. The launch
transaction executed the one-way metadata freeze, so the base URIs are
immutable; the website reads and displays that onchain state.

## 2. What QUOTRONS is

QUOTRONS is a 4,444-terminal ERC-404 collection. The NFTs are pixel-art
renderings of machines that carried stock prices through history, from
an 1814 semaphore to a 1971 dealer terminal.

One whole liquid QUOTRON materializes one dark terminal. Selling below a
whole liquid unit dissolves a dark terminal back into circulation.
Burning the one-token backing of a dark terminal hardwires it:

- the burn is permanent and irreversible;
- the terminal becomes a permanent ERC-721;
- its metadata changes permanently from dark art to animated lit art;
- it no longer dissolves when liquid QUOTRON is sold; and
- its owner can claim promotional stock rewards.

V2 preserves the invariant:

  liquid ERC-20 supply + hardwired terminals × 1e18 = 4,444e18

There is no NFT mint sale. Whole liquid units materialize dark terminals.

## 3. The machines

The four standard tiers and reward weights are:

- Quotron I Keypad (1960): 2,450 total, 1.0x weight.
- Quotron II Desk Unit (1962): 1,330 total, 1.5x weight.
- Quotron 800 (1970s): 530 total, 2.5x weight.
- NASDANK Terminal (1971): 130 total, 5.0x weight.

Each of the ten reward tracks has the same 245 / 133 / 53 / 13 standard
tier mix. The remaining four IDs are one-of-one relics:

- #4441, The Semaphore (1814): one third of the 12.5% basket carve from
  every reward stock, equal to about 0.0833% of steady-state volume.
- #4442, The Universal Ticker (1871): the same basket share.
- #4443, The Trans-Lux (1923): the same basket share.
- #4444, The Gold Indicator (1867): 5% of each reward-stock inflow,
  equal to 0.10% of steady-state volume.

Relic reward pots can accrue before hardwiring, but only a hardwired
relic owner can claim them.

## 4. One trading market, ten reward stocks

V2 does not trade against ten stocks in ten QUOTRON pools. It has one
canonical QUOTRON/WETH Uniswap v4 pool.

The ten tokenized-stock markets are reward conversion routes:

NVDA, AAPL, TSLA, GME, SPCX, SPY, PLTR, NFLX, RDDT, and MSTR.

Every standard terminal is assigned permanently to one reward track.
Fresh WETH reward fees are converted to those stocks in bounded epochs.
The resulting stock is deposited into the reflections engine, where
hardwired terminals claim it directly.

The Gold Indicator accrues the ten stock tokens directly. V2 has no
fresh PAXG conversion rail. The zero-action distribution did not move
accrued V1 rewards into V2.

## 5. Fee policy

The permanent base fee is 3% of WETH-side volume. At that rate:

- 2.0000% of volume funds hardwired-terminal stock rewards;
- 0.6375% compounds protocol-owned locked QUOTRON liquidity;
- 0.2125% buys and burns STONKBROKERS; and
- 0.1500% goes to the creator.

The hook first collects WETH. Destination-locked contracts then process
the three protocol pots; no caller can redirect their recipient.

Before one-way launch-fee finalization:

- the keeper pays 3%;
- wallets accepted by the published eligibility registry pay 3%;
- STONK BROKER holders qualify through the registry's live collection
  check and pay 3%;
- other public wallets pay 90%; and
- a recipient that actually pays the 90% buy fee is transfer-restricted
  for one hour.

The owner manually finalizes launch fees after the reviewed launch
sequence. Finalization is irreversible and makes the fee 3% for
everyone. That finalization has executed. There is no six-hour timer and
no 40% V2 tier.

## 6. Stock reward epochs

At the 3% base fee, two points of every WETH-side trade enter the hook's
reflection pot. The epoch converter:

- opens only after at least 0.1 WETH is available;
- is capped at 100 WETH per epoch;
- enforces at least 60 seconds between epochs;
- splits each opened WETH budget equally across all ten reward tracks;
- uses sealed WETH -> USDG -> stock routes; and
- sends each converted stock only to the V2 reflections contract.

The Machine's Record uses cumulative onchain accounting rather than
displaying the live reflection pot as lifetime earnings. It adds every
historical reward-pot pull to the current queue, then separately shows
WETH already converted and allocated, WETH in an active conversion, and
WETH waiting for the next epoch. The displayed USD equivalent uses the
current ETH/USD mark, not a historical per-epoch exchange rate.

For each track's converted stock:

- 82.5% goes to ordinary hardwired terminals on that track by live
  reward weight;
- 12.5% goes to the three basket relics, one third each; and
- 5% goes to the Gold Indicator.

Within an ordinary reward track:

  share = terminal live weight / total active track weight

Tier weights are 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 5.0. If the terminal owner holds a
STONK BROKER NFT, the terminal's live weight is 1.25x. Anyone may call
poke(id) after the owner's Broker status changes; accounting settles at
the previous weight before repricing.

Claims are terminal-ID based. Pending rewards travel with a hardwired NFT
when it transfers. A seller may claim immediately before a sale, so
buyers should re-check pending balances at execution time.

## 7. Canonical routing enforcement

The only supported market route is:

  user -> QuotronWethRouter -> registered QUOTRON/WETH v4 pool

Enforcement exists at multiple layers:

- the hook rejects swaps unless the sender is the immutable canonical
  router;
- the hook rejects any pool except the registered pool;
- the QUOTRON token requires one-shot PoolManager settlement
  authorization issued by that hook;
- the token verifies that authorization was consumed;
- known non-v4 AMM venue code hashes can be banned; and
- EOA code hashes cannot be banned, preserving wallet-to-wallet use.

This makes an alternate v4 pool or direct PoolManager settlement unable
to bypass the canonical fee hook. Known non-v4 venue codehashes can be
blocked, but ordinary ERC-20 transfers remain possible; this is not a
claim that every arbitrary future custom contract is categorically
impossible. The canonical router supports:

  buyExactEth(minQuotronOut, recipient, deadline) payable
    -> quotronOut

  sellExactQuotronForEth(quotronIn, minEthOut, recipient, deadline)
    -> ethOut

Sells require ERC-20 approval to the canonical router. Approve only the
amount needed. Aggregators should eth_call QuotronWethQuoter with
(amount, payer). That returns output and the payer's fee without the
payer holding ETH, QUOTRON, or a router allowance. Execution still
goes through the canonical router with an explicit slippage minimum.
Exact-output buys are unavailable when the payer's fee is 50% or higher.
There is no hosted quote API key.

## 8. V1 to V2 distribution

The V2 distribution used a published snapshot and Merkle commitment.
It was executed without requiring users to sign a migration transaction.

The ledger preserved exact terminal IDs and hardwired state where
eligible, rerolled liquid allocations according to the published plan,
and included reviewed restitution replacements. V1 purchases on any
venue after the applicable cutoff were not honored: OpenSea, unofficial
external pools, or direct transfers. Dark ownership, liquid balances,
and fractional balances froze at halt block 34984482; lit copies for
terminals already hardwired at that block followed V1 NFT ownership
through block 35337540.

The migration reserve was capped to the committed ledger. Distribution
could finalize only after:

- total distributed amount equaled the cap; and
- the migration reserve held zero QUOTRON.

Finalization permanently closes all remaining claim, assignment,
handoff, fractional, and restitution functions. The website's V1
Migration Record is now a read-only audit view, except that users can
still revoke unsafe V1 operator approvals.

The zero-action distribution copied eligible V2 IDs and hardwired state
without taking the V1 NFTs. It emitted no legacy-reward migration events,
so accrued V1 rewards remain separate V1 entitlements; V2 terminals earn
new rewards from V2 epochs.

The final V1 historical rewards record is $113,849.84 in
tokenized-stock rewards generated for hardwired holders. The site rounds
this to $113,850 for display. The archive derives it from the final
onchain V1 burn pots, the contract's exact 80/17 reward-to-burn fee
ratio, and the frozen stock marks used for final V1 accounting. It is
not a live claimable balance or a current-market-price valuation. The
machine-readable calculation is served at:

  /migration/v1-historical-rewards.json

## 9. V2 security and disclosed controls

V2 was designed after the V1 stale-approval incident. It does not rely
on the V1 mirror for V2 ownership or approvals.

The V2 mirror clears an NFT's individual approval whenever a
base-driven ownership or approval-domain transition occurs, including
materialization and hardwiring. Wallet-wide operator approvals remain
explicit choices controlled by the wallet owner.

Disclosed emergency controls exist:

- the owner can pause the core and hook, ban or unban non-EOA venue
  codehashes, and irreversibly finalize the public fee at 3%;
- the blacklist guardian can freeze addresses but cannot remove a freeze;
- the recovery administrator can add or remove freezes;
- the recovery administrator is a Safe with a threshold of at least two;
- that Safe can forcibly move V2 ERC-20 balances or terminal NFTs
  between eligible non-protocol accounts;
- protected protocol accounts cannot be arbitrary recovery targets; and
- every freeze and recovery action emits an onchain event.

These controls are trust assumptions, not decentralization claims.
Metadata base URIs and the ERC721-C transfer validator become immutable
at launch.

## 10. NFT transfers and creator earnings

The V2 mirror uses ERC721-C transfer validation. The intended creator
earning is 5% of an NFT sale:

- 4% to the keeper; and
- 1% to the creator.

Marketplace support depends on whether the venue honors the configured
transfer validator and required payment split. Buyers and sellers must
verify venue compatibility. This NFT creator earning is separate from
the 0.15% creator carve on canonical token-market volume.

## 13. Common questions

Is V2 the same OpenSea collection as V1?

No. The V2 mirror is a new ERC-721 contract and appears as a separate
collection. V1 remains visible as historical onchain state.

Why did V2 NFTs appear without a migration transaction?

The reviewed genesis distributor delivered the committed allocations
directly. Users did not need to claim.

Can I trade now?

Only when the V2 core reports launched and neither the core nor the
canonical hook is paused. The website displays those onchain states.

Do dark terminals earn?

Ordinary dark terminals do not participate in stock rewards. Relic pots
can accrue before hardwiring, but cannot be claimed until the relic is
hardwired.

Can I undo hardwiring?

No. Hardwiring burns one liquid token permanently.

Can I choose a reward stock or machine?

No. Assignment is deterministic and terminal creation draws from the
available ID pool. Selling below a whole unit dissolves a dark terminal;
crossing back above a whole unit can draw another available ID.

What creates rewards?

Canonical QUOTRON/WETH trading fees. WETH is converted to the ten stocks
by the sealed epoch converter. NFT marketplace creator earnings do not
fund the V2 stock reward pots.

Is QUOTRONS affiliated with Robinhood, NASDAQ, Quotron, or Trans-Lux?

No. It is an independent project using historical machines as subject
matter. Living trademarks are parodied; defunct brands are referenced
as historical facts.

## 14. Risks

- Smart contracts, wallets, marketplaces, RPCs, and a young chain can
  fail.
- The canonical market can be extremely volatile and illiquid.
- A 90% launch-phase fee is intentionally punitive; always read the
  onchain fee for the actual payer before signing. A recipient that
  actually pays it is transfer-restricted for one hour.
- Hardwiring is irreversible.
- Reward volume may never materialize.
- Tokenized stocks are third-party upgradeable assets whose issuer can
  pause or change them.
- The blacklist guardian and recovery Safe are explicit trust
  assumptions.
- Canonical v4 settlement is enforced and known venue codehashes can be
  blocked, but arbitrary future custom contracts cannot be represented
  as categorically impossible.
- ERC721-C marketplace compatibility varies; unsupported venues may
  reject NFT transfers or listings.
- Stock-token access is restricted in some jurisdictions, including the
  United States region.
- Never risk more than you can afford to lose completely.
