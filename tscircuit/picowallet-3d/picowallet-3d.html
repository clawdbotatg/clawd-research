What changed

  • Zero soldering, for real. The screen is the Waveshare 1.3" LCD Module (45 × 31 mm, $9.49), which ships with an 8-wire cable and the plug already on it. Our board has the matching JST-PH socket (LCSC C157974, JLCPCB solders it) and four standoff holes on the module's 40 × 26 mm hole grid. The battery plug is the same story.
  • All 8 screen signals wired: VCC GND DIN CLK CS DC RST BL, same GPIOs the firmware uses today.
  • Under the screen: a 402030 LiPo (20 × 30 × 4 mm, about 200 mAh) standing tall. ATECC608B moved to the top-left corner above the joystick, charger to the bottom-right under the B button. Power switch pokes out the bottom edge. Back: only the Pico 2 W, USB out the left edge.
  • v0.5: power switch flipped so its knob hangs past the bottom edge. Two lights beside it: green PWR (on whenever the board has power) and red CHG (on while the battery charges, driven by the charger chip). Board: 72 × 44 mm, about 17 mm thick with 6 mm standoffs. Routed, no shorts, placement clean.

Will it work

80%chance the first batch runs the firmware with no board fix. Every net re-checked from the compiled netlist on 2026-09-23: Pico mirror and all 40 labels, joystick, ATECC608B SOIC-8 pinout, TP4056 pinout, diode direction, switch common pin, 7-pin screen header. Nobody has powered one.
  • Pico footprint on the back: checked. Pin 1 lands top-right and USB right when you look at the back, which is what a Pico looks like with its USB on the right. All 40 pin labels match the official pinout.
  • Joystick: checked. ALPS datasheet: pin 2 is center push, pin 5 is common. Both wired right. Its four directions run on the diagonals, so it now sits at 45° like on the Waveshare board. Which direction is which is a firmware table, and the firmware already has a key-test screen for that.
  • Screen pin order taken from the Waveshare manual: VCC GND DIN CLK CS DC RST BL. Socket pin 1 is marked on our silkscreen; the cable only fits one way.
  • Power switch: checked by geometry. Pad 2 sits between pads 1 and 3, the common on every SPDT slide switch of this type, and pad 2 is the one wired to the battery. The LCSC datasheet PDF would not download, so this is by convention and geometry, not a drawing.
  • WiFi. The Pico's antenna is at its right end, under the B button and the battery plug. Expect some range loss, not a dead radio.
  • No battery protection on the board. Buy LiPos with a built-in protection board. Charge current set to about 235 mA (5.1k PROG resistor), right for a 200 mAh cell.
  • Thickness. About 20 mm: screen stack 14 mm on the front, Pico 4 mm on the back. The battery adds nothing.
  • Not electrically tested. Passed tscircuit netlist, placement and shorts checks, plus a manual net-by-net review against datasheets. Nobody has powered one. JLCPCB runs its own manufacturing check at upload.
